Privacy policy

BMI Calculator: Body Fat & BMR (Android, package bmi.calculator.bmicalculator.bodymass.bodymassindex) · Effective 8 October 2026

In short: everything you enter (your measurements, results, history and photos) stays in the app's private storage on your phone, and only leaves it when you choose to export or share it, or through Android's own backup to your Google account. The app shows ads from Google AdMob, and reports app usage and crashes to Google so we can see what is working and what is broken. Those services receive device and usage information — never your measurements, your results or your photos.

Who we are

The app is developed and published on Google Play by Asad Ullah Sansi ("we", "us"). For any question about privacy or this policy, email asadullahsansi@gmail.com.

What the app stores on your phone

The app handles personal and health information that you choose to enter:

  • Details about each person: name, sex, age, height, weight, activity level, chosen comparison standard, and a calorie goal if you save one.
  • Optional body measurements (neck, waist, hip), pregnancy details, and resting heart rate, if you add them.
  • Your logged history, such as weights and measurements, and the results of calculations you run.
  • Progress photos, if you add any.
  • App settings, such as units and theme.

This information is used only to calculate and show your results inside the app. It is never sent to us, never given to the advertising service, and never used for advertising or profiling.

What we collect

The app has no account and no sign-up, and we never receive your measurements, your results, your history or your photos. We do use two Google services that report on how the app is used and whether it is crashing. Neither of them is ever given anything about your body.

App analytics

Firebase Analytics, a service of Google LLC, records which parts of the app are used, so we can tell whether a change helped or hurt. It is limited to a fixed list of actions:

  • finishing first-time setup, with how long it took, grouped into broad bands, and whether you use metric or imperial units;
  • opening a calculator, with which calculator and which comparison standard is selected;
  • logging a weight — that one was logged, never the weight itself. The count is grouped as "1", "2–5" or "6 or more";
  • changing your comparison standard;
  • exporting a CSV or PDF;
  • being shown the "rate the app" prompt.

That list is the whole of it. No weight, height, BMI, body-fat figure, waist measurement, age, name or photo is sent, and the app contains no way to send one: the events are defined in code as a fixed set, so a measurement cannot be attached to one even by mistake.

Google also records standard information with these events, such as country, device model, operating system version, app version and language. We do not ask Firebase for your advertising ID.

Crash reports

Firebase Crashlytics, also a service of Google LLC, sends a report when the app crashes: what the app was doing at the time, the device model and the operating system version. This is how we find and fix faults. Crash reports contain no information you entered.

Your choice

Both are switched off until consent is settled. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, nothing is collected until you answer the consent form the app shows on first run, and you can change that answer at any time in Profile → Ad privacy choices. The same single choice covers analytics, crash reports and ads — we do not ask twice or count you through a second route.

Every calculation still works offline, with or without consent. Nothing in the app is withheld for refusing.

Rating the app

After you have used the app for a while, Google Play may show its own "rate this app" dialog inside the app. The dialog, the star rating and any review you write belong to Google Play, not to us: we never see who was asked, who rated, or what anyone wrote, and nothing you entered in the app is attached to it. The app only ever records, on your phone, that the dialog has been shown once so you are not asked again.

Advertising

The app is free and shows a small banner ad on three screens (Calculators, History and Profile). Ads never appear on your results, on a calculator, or next to your photos. The ads are provided by Google AdMob, a service of Google LLC.

To show and measure ads, and to prevent fraud, the Google Mobile Ads SDK in the app collects and sends to Google:

  • your device's advertising ID;
  • your IP address, which Google may use to estimate your approximate location (country or city, not precise location);
  • device and app information, such as phone model, operating system, language and app version;
  • how you interact with ads, such as views and taps, and diagnostic information such as crash and performance data from the ad service.

The app never gives Google your name, age, sex, measurements, results, history, photos or anything else you enter. One exception keeps younger users safer: if the age saved in the app is under 18, the app asks Google to treat ad requests as coming from a minor. Only that yes-or-no is sent, not the age. All ads are limited to content suitable for teenagers.

How Google uses this information is described in Google's policy at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites. Data is sent over an encrypted connection.

Your ad choices

  • European Economic Area, UK and Switzerland: the app asks for your consent before any personalised ad is shown. You can change your choice at any time in Profile → Ad privacy choices. Where consent rules apply in other regions, including some US states, the same option appears.
  • Everywhere: you can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads, in your phone's settings (Settings → Privacy → Ads, or Settings → Google → Ads, depending on the phone).

The app uses the internet only to fetch ads. Every calculator works fully offline; without a connection, no ad is shown.

Permissions

The app asks you for no permissions. There is no prompt to accept or refuse. The Google services it uses need a few that Android grants automatically:

  • internet and network access, used by ads, analytics and crash reports;
  • the advertising ID and the related ad-services permissions, used by the advertising service;
  • background scheduling, used by the ad service to prepare requests off the main thread;
  • permission to read the Google Play install referrer, which tells the analytics service how the app was found — for example which listing or campaign led to the install. It carries no information about you.

When you add a photo, you choose it with Android's own photo picker, and the app receives only that picture. The app never asks for the camera, your location, your contacts, your files or your gallery.

How your data is protected

  • Everything is kept in the app's private storage, which Android's app sandbox keeps out of reach of other apps.
  • Photos are stored inside the app, not in your gallery.
  • On phones where device encryption is on (the default on modern Android), the stored data is encrypted along with the rest of the device.

Backup to your Google account

So that a new phone does not lose your record, the app takes part in Android's built-in backup. Android copies your measurements, history, people and app settings to your own Google account, and restores them when you set up a new device or reinstall the app.

This is Android's feature, not ours. The copy is held in your Google account, under Google's terms, and we have no access to it and never receive it.

  • Progress photos are never backed up. They stay on the device they were taken on, and are lost if you reset the phone without exporting them yourself.
  • You can turn backup off for all apps in Android settings, under System → Backup. Turning it off does not affect anything stored in the app.
  • Google's handling of backups is described at policies.google.com/privacy.

Exporting, importing and sharing

Data leaves the app only when you ask it to:

  • Export writes a CSV or PDF file to a location you pick.
  • Import reads a backup file you pick.
  • Share hands an image of your result to the app you choose from Android's share menu.

What happens to an exported or shared file after that is up to you and the app or service you send it to.

How long data is kept, and how to delete it

Your data stays on your phone until you delete it or uninstall the app. We never hold a copy, so there is nothing for us to keep, retrieve or delete on our side. A backup held in your own Google account is kept and removed under Google's terms, and you can delete it from your Google account's backup settings. Information collected by Google for advertising, analytics and crash reports is kept under Google's own retention policy; you can reset your advertising ID in your phone's settings, and withdraw consent at any time in Profile → Ad privacy choices.

  • Profile → Delete my data permanently removes everything the app has stored, for every person.
  • Profile → Remove this person permanently removes one person and everything they logged.
  • History → any entry can be deleted on its own, and Clear history removes all of it.
  • Uninstalling the app removes all of its data.

Children

The app is intended for people aged 13 and over. For anyone under 20, it shows growth percentiles instead of adult BMI categories, and it does not offer calorie or weight-loss plans. Its child mode is designed for parents checking a child's growth percentile. The app is not directed at children under 13. Ads are limited to content suitable for teenagers, and when the saved age is under 18, ad requests are marked as coming from a minor, which turns off personalised ads.

Health information

This app is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition. Talk to a healthcare professional about your results.
